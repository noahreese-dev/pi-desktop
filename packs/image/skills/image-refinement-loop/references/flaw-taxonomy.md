# Visual flaw taxonomy

Use this as a search list, not as permission to invent requirements. Apply only
the modules relevant to the image and its controlling evidence.

## 1. Global image checks

- Correct subject, plan, unit, room, route segment, time, and intended use.
- Correct orientation, handedness, sides, counts, labels, and identity.
- Camera height, field of view, horizon, verticals, crop, and lens distortion.
- Foreground, midground, and background agree spatially.
- Focal subject is not accidentally cropped, hidden, merged, or duplicated.
- Negative space, visual balance, exposure, white balance, contrast, and depth.
- No important defect hidden by bloom, darkness, shallow focus, or furniture.

## 2. Geometry and topology

- Wall lengths, thicknesses, corners, ceiling heights, floor levels, and slopes.
- Room shape, adjacency, openings, circulation loops, and destination of doors.
- Required elements exist once; forbidden or unsupported elements do not exist.
- Parallel lines, perpendicular joins, symmetry, spacing, grids, and alignments.
- Furniture and fixtures do not silently change the apparent room dimensions.
- Perspective does not create a false width, depth, ceiling height, or opening.
- No wall dissolves, intersects furniture, changes thickness, or ends impossibly.

## 3. Human use and ergonomics

- A person can enter, turn, pass, sit, stand, reach, wash, cook, park, and leave.
- Doors, drawers, appliances, closets, chairs, and vehicle doors can open.
- Walkways and working clearances are plausible for the stated room size.
- Handles, switches, faucets, mirrors, counters, seating, and storage are at
  believable heights and orientations.
- Headroom, landing depth, guard heights, stair rhythm, and handrail continuity.
- Cleaning and maintenance access is plausible; nothing essential is trapped.
- Camera position could physically exist or is honestly treated as a removed
  architectural-visualization camera.

## 4. Architectural systems

### Doors and openings

- Correct number, wall, side, swing, hinge, handle, frame, threshold, and reveal.
- Door swing does not collide with a fixture, wall, stair, cabinet, or another door.
- Openings have believable destinations and consistent wall depth.

### Stairs and guards

- Steps form one continuous climb with consistent tread/riser proportions.
- Stair begins and ends at actual floor levels; landing and turn match the plan.
- No repeated flight, hidden descent, impossible underside, or missing opening.
- Rails, guards, posts, balusters, nosings, and headroom behave continuously.

### Windows and exterior openings

- Correct count, wall, size, sill/head height, mullions, depth, and exterior view.
- Daylight direction agrees with visible windows and time of day.
- No window appears on a party wall or other prohibited elevation.

### Kitchen and millwork

- Cabinet modules, doors, drawers, toe kicks, fillers, corners, and countertop edges.
- Sink, faucet, cooktop, oven, hood, fridge, dishwasher, outlets, and clearances.
- Appliance doors can open; island seating and work aisles are usable.
- Plumbing and ventilation locations make sense relative to walls and floors.

### Bathrooms

- Correct toilet, vanity, sink, tub, shower, mirror, door, and window count.
- Fixtures stay on plan-controlled walls and have usable clearances.
- Faucet aligns with basin; vanity has plausible depth; toilet sits on the floor.
- Mirror reflects the actual opposing scene, not a blank panel or invented room.
- Wet surfaces, glass, drains, grout, waterproof transitions, and lighting behave.

### Garage and driveway

- Garage door width/height, bay count, wall depth, slab, tracks, opener, and access.
- Vehicle scale, turning path, walking clearance, and door-opening space.
- Driveway meets the garage slab and street with a usable grade and drainage.
- No curb, planter, step, or landscaping blocks the vehicle route.

### Electrical, mechanical, and plumbing clues

- Lights, switches, outlets, vents, registers, drains, and fixtures are plausible.
- Repeated lights align; vents do not float; plumbing does not occupy impossible space.

## 5. Optical and lighting behavior

- One consistent camera model and perspective across the frame.
- Mirrors show the correct opposing surfaces at the correct angle and scale.
- Glass has coherent transmission, reflections, thickness, edges, and occlusion.
- Polished metal, stone, vehicles, and screens reflect the same environment.
- Light sources explain highlights and shadows; shadow directions agree.
- Contact shadows ground objects; nothing floats or glows without a source.
- Exterior brightness, interior exposure, and window views share one time/weather.
- No unexplained grey mirror, black window, fake portal, or pasted reflection.

## 6. Materials and construction

- Materials have believable scale, thickness, seams, grain, veining, and repetition.
- Tile/grout, flooring boards, brick, panels, trim, baseboards, and casing terminate.
- Wood grain and stone veining do not stretch, melt, mirror, or change direction.
- Corners, edges, reveals, joints, hardware, caulk lines, and transitions are buildable.
- Repeated textures do not visibly clone or cross unrelated surfaces.
- Furniture and fixtures contact floors/walls correctly and have adequate support.

## 7. Object and scene semantics

- Each object has a clear function, correct orientation, and plausible placement.
- Object counts and identities remain stable; no fused, half-formed, or extra objects.
- Decor does not masquerade as a window, door, stair opening, or structural element.
- Plants, curtains, rugs, art, lamps, dishes, towels, and accessories obey gravity.
- Vehicles have complete wheels, lights, windows, doors, reflections, and scale.
- People, if present, have plausible anatomy, gaze, grip, contact, and interaction.
- Text, numbers, signs, controls, logos, and labels are correct or deliberately absent.

## 8. Continuity across a series

- Same plan, unit condition, route, room adjacency, and camera direction.
- Same door/window locations, materials, hardware, ceiling, trim, and lighting logic.
- Persistent objects keep identity, scale, color, orientation, and position.
- First/end frames agree with neighboring clips or images without a spatial jump.
- Window views, weather, daylight, landscaping, and exterior orientation remain stable.
- No room is invented, removed, mirrored, enlarged, or moved between adjacent views.

## 9. Generator artifacts

- Duplicated handles, faucets, lights, legs, cushions, fixtures, doors, or windows.
- Melted edges, fused objects, broken circles, wavy lines, impossible intersections.
- Floating objects, missing supports, penetrations, tangencies, or detached shadows.
- Fake text, watermarks, signatures, logos, compression, halos, or sharpening artifacts.
- Over-smoothed surfaces, plastic materials, repeated texture stamps, or AI symmetry.
- Convenient blur or darkness hiding a required architectural fact.

## 10. Final presentation quality

- The image remains readable at intended desktop/mobile crop and display size.
- Important geometry is visible, not merely claimed in accompanying text.
- Detail is sufficient for the intended review without false documentary certainty.
- Styling supports the project reference without overriding plan-controlled facts.
- The result feels coherent at first glance and survives close inspection.

## Severity rule

- Mark source conflicts, wrong topology, identity, count, or destination as **hard**.
- Mark physics, use, optics, construction, continuity, and generator failures as
  **material** even when no drawing explicitly mentions them.
- Mark only non-blocking color, crop, styling, or polish differences as **cosmetic**.
- Mark unknowable facts as **unresolved** and omit them rather than guessing.

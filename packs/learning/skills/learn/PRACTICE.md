# Practice: make it last

Feeling fluent right after a lesson is not the same as remembering it next month. What lasts is built by effortful
recall, spread out over time and mixed with other material. These practices feel harder than re-reading, and that
difficulty is the point, as long as the learner can still succeed most of the time.

## Retrieval practice

- Ask them to **produce** the idea from memory: explain it, use it, write the steps, answer a question. Re-reading
  and highlighting feel productive but build little.
- **Before** showing any notes. Recall first, then check against the lesson.
- Low stakes: practice is for learning, not grading. Say so.
- Always give feedback after the attempt, with the reason, so errors are not rehearsed.

## Spacing

- Review a new idea again after about a day, then a few days, then a week or two, then a month. Stretch the gap when
  recall is easy; shorten it when it fails.
- Use the learning records' dates and the lessons' numbers to see what is due. When they open a session, a short
  warm-up on something due is a good start.
- A few minutes of spaced review beats a long cram session.

## Interleaving

- Once two or more related ideas or problem types are learned, mix them in one set instead of practising each in a
  block. Having to choose the right method is part of the skill.
- Mix only material that has been taught; interleaving brand-new ideas just confuses.

## Desirable difficulty, not discouragement

- Aim for questions they get right most of the time but not all of the time. All correct: make it harder or space it
  out. Mostly wrong: step back, re-teach with a new example, then practise again.
- Vary the surface: different numbers, contexts and wordings, so they learn the idea and not the example.
- Ask for explanations ("why does this work?") as well as answers.

## Formats

- **In chat:** one question at a time, wait, feedback, next.
- **As a page:** a practice lesson in `lessons/NNNN-practice-<slug>.html` with self-grading questions and
  explanations, linking back to the lessons it reviews.

## Afterwards

Record what the session showed: ideas now held reliably, ideas that failed recall (they're due again soon),
misconceptions that resurfaced. Update the learning records, not a log. Tell the learner, honestly and briefly, what
is solid and what needs another round.
